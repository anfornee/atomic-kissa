# Experience principles

Atomic Kissa should feel more like entering a place than operating a website. Atmosphere, pacing, silence, and the sense of inhabiting a room matter as much as navigation. The interface can be sparse because negative space is part of the composition, not an area waiting to be filled.

Transitions belong to the experience. A form arriving, dissolving, drifting, reorganizing, or reforming can communicate a change of state more naturally than a conventional page swap. Slow movement is welcome when the waiting itself carries mood or meaning. Input acknowledgement, however, should remain immediate so deliberate pacing never resembles a missed click or a stalled application.

The current motion character is calm, suspended, organic, and slightly ceremonial. It suggests matter moving through a quiet room rather than effects being triggered for display. Stillness remains important: not every surface needs motion, and completed moments should have room to breathe.

The visual world currently draws from modern minimalism, atomic and space-age forms, Japanese composition, and late-night listening-room atmosphere. These references are ingredients rather than a theme to illustrate literally. The result should feel refined and contemporary, with restraint carrying more weight than ornament.

Conventional web patterns are not forbidden, but they should earn their place. A familiar control is useful when it improves clarity, accessibility, or confidence. Cards, panels, dashboards, and dense navigation should not become defaults simply because they are common.

Avoid motion that feels flashy, game-like, hyperactive, or designed mainly to prove a technical effect. The work is strongest when the mechanics recede and the visitor notices a change in atmosphere.

These principles describe the current direction. Future experiments are encouraged when they preserve the project’s sense of place, attention, and restraint—even if the specific visuals or transitions evolve.
