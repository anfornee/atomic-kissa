# Experience principles

**Status: Approved working direction**

## Vision

Atomic Kissa is a digital Japanese jazz-kissa and listening-bar experience: a quiet threshold into coffee, music, editorial discovery, and “other worlds.” It should feel more like entering a place than operating a website.

Its character brings together modern Japanese minimalism, mid-century atomic-age design, deep navy and black, warm gold and orange light, orbital imagery, generous negative space, and slow, contemplative motion. These are ingredients, not a literal theme or a fixed design system.

The supporting line is:

> Coffee · Jazz · Other Worlds

## Product principles

### Atmosphere has a purpose

Pacing, silence, negative space, light, and motion should help visitors pay attention. The mechanics should recede; visitors should notice a change in atmosphere rather than a demonstration of effects.

### Unusual does not mean unclear

The experience may be cinematic and surprising while retaining recognizable navigation, immediate feedback, real URLs, and clear hierarchy. Familiar patterns are welcome when they improve access, confidence, or comprehension.

### Deliberate pacing still acknowledges input

Slow transformations are appropriate when the waiting carries mood or meaning. Every input should nevertheless receive immediate visual or semantic acknowledgement so intentional pacing never resembles lag or a missed action.

### Stillness is part of the composition

Not every surface needs movement. Settled states need room to breathe, and content should remain satisfying when animation is unavailable or reduced.

### Content and art direction belong together

Entries are not interchangeable cards. Editorial content may carry a curated palette, composition, imagery, and motion profile, but those choices come from a controlled design vocabulary maintained in code. The CMS supplies art direction; it does not become an unrestricted page builder.

### Accessibility is part of the atmosphere

Keyboard access, assistive-technology semantics, legible contrast, responsive layouts, focus behavior, and reduced motion are core qualities. The intended tone must survive without animation, precision pointing, audio, or a large screen.

### Mobile is a first-class room

Narrow viewports are not reduced desktop. Composition, reading order, hit targets, media treatment, performance, and motion should be designed and tested from mobile outward.

## Initial product scope

The first public experience is intentionally simple:

- Home
- Listen
- Drink
- Explore
- About
- Individual entry pages

The first operational scope is a single owner managing entries, media, and site settings through a purpose-built CMS. The system should leave clean seams for additional staff roles and public accounts without treating those future capabilities as launch requirements.

## Exploratory experience concepts

The following ideas are promising but not approved release requirements:

- A homepage that functions as a quiet threshold rather than a conventional content wall.
- An atmospheric archive paired with an accessible list or grid view.
- “Side A” as an immersive listening room and “Side B” as notes, credits, track lists, and pairings.
- Scroll-reactive changes in color, light, scale, and atmosphere driven by ordinary vertical scrolling.
- Particle-led transitions and spatial continuity between selected content and its destination.

These concepts should be tested only after the standard public routes work well on their own. See [Interaction guidelines](interaction-guidelines.md) and the [Roadmap](roadmap.md).

## Out of initial scope

Public profiles, personalization, saved listening sessions, event registration, memberships, community features, and multi-role editorial workflows are deferred. Their future possibility should influence boundaries—not inflate the first release.
