# Visual direction

**Status: Approved working direction**

Atomic Kissa lives in deep navy and near-black environments with warm gold, amber, and orange light. Cream or off-white typography can soften reading surfaces. Contrast should feel nocturnal and luminous rather than harsh, while still meeting the legibility needs of text, controls, and focus indicators.

## Composition

Composition is minimalist and mobile-first. Generous negative space gives each element presence and lets small changes register without competition. Alignment, rhythm, scale, and spacing should remain deliberate even when a scene appears nearly empty.

Asymmetry and intervals can create tension and calm, but reading order and control placement must remain understandable. Dense panels, dashboards, and uniform card grids should not become defaults merely because they are familiar application patterns.

## Form and imagery

Atomic, orbital, and celestial forms—rings, points, paths, fields, and measured arcs—are natural references. They work best as quiet structural motifs, not literal science-fiction decoration.

Photography and artwork should feel selected rather than filled in. Backgrounds, hero images, crops, and focal points can differ by entry, provided content remains legible and the composition has a reliable fallback when media is absent or fails to load.

The Japanese influence is primarily compositional: restraint, asymmetry, intervals, material sensitivity, and attention to emptiness. It should not depend on decorative clichés. Restrained retro-futurism and moody listening-room light can add warmth without turning the interface into a period pastiche.

## Typography

Typography should feel refined and intentional. Letter spacing, line weight, measure, and placement are part of the atmosphere. Custom geometry can be useful when type participates in a transformation, but editorial text should use robust, selectable, and readable typography.

## Controlled variation

Entries may select from named, tested options for:

- Palettes
- Background treatments
- Hero compositions
- Image placement and crop behavior
- Editorial section variants
- Motion profiles

These choices form a curated vocabulary owned by the application. Arbitrary colors, CSS, scripts, and unrestricted layout controls do not belong in the CMS. Each option needs responsive, contrast, missing-media, and reduced-motion behavior before it becomes editorially available.

The vocabulary may grow with the work; it should not be made abstract in anticipation of every possible composition. See [CMS and content model](cms-and-content-model.md).

## Avoid

- Literal spaceship interfaces and glowing control panels
- Dense data-display aesthetics
- Ornamental overload or uncontrolled particle glow
- Decorative Japanese motifs used without context
- Motion or typography that compromises reading
- Visual variants that have no reliable responsive fallback

This direction is a shared world, not a locked palette. New work should be judged by whether it belongs in that quiet world, not whether it copies an existing screen.
