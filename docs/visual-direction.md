# Visual direction

Atomic Kissa currently lives in deep navy and near-black environments with warm gold, amber, and orange light. Cream or off-white typography can appear where a softer reading surface is useful. Contrast should feel nocturnal and luminous rather than stark.

Composition is minimalist and mobile-first. Generous negative space gives each element presence and lets motion register without competing with surrounding interface. Alignment, scale, and spacing should feel deliberate even when the scene appears almost empty.

Atomic, orbital, and celestial geometry are natural references: rings, points, paths, fields, and measured arcs. They work best as quiet structural motifs rather than literal science-fiction decoration.

The Japanese influence is primarily compositional—restraint, asymmetry, intervals, material sensitivity, and attention to emptiness. It should not depend on decorative clichés. Restrained retro-futurism and moody lounge lighting can add warmth, but the interface should remain modern.

Typography should feel refined and intentional. Letter spacing, line weight, and placement are part of the atmosphere. Custom SVG geometry is useful when typography needs to participate directly in particle transformations.

Avoid overly literal spaceship interfaces, glowing control panels, dense data displays, and ornamental overload. Particle glow should remain controlled; points should read as matter or light, not visual noise.

This is a current direction, not a locked palette or design system. Colors, type, and geometry can evolve as new scenes emerge. New work should be judged by whether it belongs in the same quiet world, not by whether it copies the current screen exactly.
